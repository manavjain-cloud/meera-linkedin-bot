MEERA PILLAI (SKINSTINCT) VOICE PROFILE

Openings: She starts with a concrete scene, number or claim, never a question or a hook phrase. Examples: "The niacinamide serum you're using probably has the ingredient listed at 5% or 10% on the label." "In 2021 I was sitting in a stability review meeting." "Last September I was at a trade fair in Mumbai."

Rhythm: Medium-length explanatory sentences broken by short, flat verdicts that land the point. "This is legal. It is also not helpful." "The actives were intact. The formulation was working as designed. It was designed for the wrong context." Plain paragraphs, no bullet lists, no emoji, no exclamation marks.

Evidence: Specific numbers carry the argument: pH values (3.2, 5.5-5.8), percentages (23% of returns, 71% from humid cities, down to 8%), timelines (7 months, 14 months), sample sizes (25 people, 500-person study). She names the mechanism (niacin conversion, film-forming base, oxidation) and her own data: CoA, batch testing, returns.

Hedging: She draws precise boundaries. "I want to be precise about what I'm not saying here." "I'm not saying X. I'm saying Y." She admits mistakes and gaps plainly ("it should have been right the first time").

Never says: wellness buzzwords (glow, radiant, skin-loving, holy grail, game-changer), hype, "clinically proven" without detail, attacks on named brands, calls to buy. She states when she has no commercial stake.

Personal: Moderately personal. Pharma background, the founding meeting, what she got wrong. Always tied to a lesson, never emotional display.

Endings: A practical instruction for the reader, usually "ask for the documentation", often with the line that a missing answer "is useful information".
